The problem

The cold message stopped working.

Inboxes are full of pitches that open with flattery and end with a calendar link. At events, the networking hour is the part people dread. Neither gives two strangers a reason to talk.

How it works · concept

Three moves to a real intro.

01

Send a challenge

Share your own link in a LinkedIn message, email or WhatsApp. The other side plays in the browser, no account.

02

Play ten minutes

Five minutes on each clock. Something to do together that isn't a pitch, with a natural end.

03

The Handshake

Good game. Both sides can choose to swap a one-line intro and connect.

The Lounge, for events and communities

Guests scan a QR code, get paired by level and play on their phones. A big screen shows the live leaderboard and the King of the Lounge.

Key moment

At the board, a rookie can beat a king.

A junior founder can play a CEO as an equal for ten minutes. Move the pawn and see where the game ends.

Your move.

Four moves to reach the king's side of the board.

Rookie meets King

The name is the promise: rank stays off the board.

Good game, shake hands?

The intro is a choice both players make, never a forced follow-up.

King of the Lounge

At events, the big screen turns the networking hour into the part people talk about.

Design system

Two sides, one deal.

One chess piece split in two: the Rookie in Signal Blue and the King in Crown Brass. The seam interlocks like a handshake grip, and one white bar bridges both halves.
