How the show works

Four moves. Zero arguments.

The wheel picks, the game keeps score, and a parent just referees. Nobody can argue with the wheel.

I

Spin the Roulette

One free spin a day. Every slice gives something: a chore, double stars, a jackpot, a freeze.

II

Splat Showdown

Pie beats spaghetti, spaghetti beats ketchup, ketchup beats pie. Best of three to dodge a chore.

III

Finish the chore

Tap done, a parent confirms, and the game releases your stars. Fair and square.

IV

Trade stars for prizes

Pizza night, screen time, a day out. Parents set the prizes, kids earn them.

Fair to a fault

The host runs the show. It never rigs it.

The game keeps score

Nobody, kid or parent, can type in points or hand out stars. Parents only confirm a chore and deliver prizes.

Little ones can win

Battles are hidden choices with no timer and nothing to aim, so an 8-year-old can beat an 18-year-old.

Chores never vanish

Lose a battle and you inherit the chore, but you still earn its stars. Daily limits mean nobody gets buried.

What I built

Brand, game and app, end to end.

The crest

A royal knight-king, a crown and a giant spoon. Purple and gold that kids read as "the big game".

The game loop

Spin, battle, finish, trade. Two currencies, streaks and specials, tuned so it stays fair at every age.

The app

A single-file web app that runs on any phone and works offline. No download, no account.

Where it is: a playable test build on the web. Not released, not in app stores yet.
