How it earns

  • Free to play. Every player, every phone, no family fee.
  • Rewarded ads, opt-in only. Watch a short ad, win a power card. Capped at 3 a day per player. Never forced, never mid-game. Today the slot is a branded reward moment labelled "Ad": no ad SDK, no ad account yet.
  • Paid extras per player: avatar skins, a season pass (more card drops, season crown), card packs. Cosmetic or convenience only. Points still come only from parent-approved chores. Prices: TBD.

Why not a family subscription: one parent decides once and usually says no. Per-player extras let each kid or teen ask for a small item, the way they already do in other games.

Rules we keep

  • No real money between kids. Swaps and counters use game points only.
  • Anti-cheat: points only after a parent approves, with a photo. The parent cannot change scores either.
  • Purchases go through the store's parent approval (Ask to Buy, Family Link).
  • Kids' ads: only SDKs approved for Google Play Families, non-personalised, no data sold. Under-13 rules (COPPA, GDPR-K) apply.

Privacy law: Israel

  • Protection of Privacy Law, 5741-1981, Amendment 13 took effect on 14 August 2025. It widens what counts as personal data, defines "information of special sensitivity", requires a Data Protection Officer for some controllers, and lets courts award damages of up to NIS 10,000 without proof of harm. The Privacy Protection Authority started enforcing it (DPO grace period ran to 31 Oct 2025).
  • What it means for Chore Wars: we keep only a nickname, avatar, badge and game scores. No email, phone, location or account. Proof photos are small (720 px), deleted on approval and auto-deleted after 7 days if never reviewed. Parents approve every new phone before it sees any family data. Join codes and PIN tries are rate-limited; PINs are stored with bcrypt.
  • Before launch: a Hebrew + English privacy notice, a named contact for data requests, and a check whether a DPO is needed at our scale (likely not: no sensitive data, no large-scale monitoring).

Ads to minors: COPPA, GDPR-K

  • Contextual or non-personalised ads only. No behavioural targeting, no ad IDs, no cross-app tracking, no retargeting. The ad sees the game, never the child.
  • COPPA (US, under 13): a persistent identifier used for targeted ads is personal information and needs verifiable parental consent. The amended COPPA Rule (in force 23 June 2025, compliance by 22 April 2026) also requires separate parental consent before sharing kids' data with third parties for advertising. We avoid this by never doing targeted ads.
  • GDPR-K (EU/UK, Article 8): under 13–16 (by country) a parent must consent for data-based services; profiling kids for marketing is discouraged (Recital 38, UK Children's Code). Same answer: no profiling, data minimised, high-privacy defaults.
  • Only ad SDKs certified for Google Play Families / Apple Kids rules, with child-directed flags set on every request.
