Who must make their website accessible in Israel, and who is exempt

I summarised from the text of the Law and the regulations who must make a website accessible, which exemptions exist and what the risks are. This is general information from reading the text, not legal advice. For your business, ask a lawyer.

Updated 14 November 2026 · Dave Markovich, 3Shamrocks Studio

General information, not legal advice. I wrote this guide from the text of the law and regulations as I checked it on 14 November 2026. Legislation changes. For your own business, ask a lawyer.

In short

  • Anyone providing a service to the public must make that service accessible, and that includes its website and app (regulations 35 to 35F).
  • The required level is IS 5568 at level AA. Part 1 of the standard is identical to WCAG 2.0, with a few national changes.
  • A VAT-exempt dealer, or anyone with average annual turnover up to ₪100,000, is exempt from these adjustments.
  • With average turnover up to ₪1 million and a site that already operated before the regulations took effect, a temporary exemption may apply, under conditions.
  • Other exemptions: technological, social networks, closed services with up to 500 registered users, and advertisements.
  • The risks: lawsuits with compensation without proof of damage, and administrative fines from the Commission (for a business with turnover up to ₪20M, a first breach until 10 Aug 2027 gets an administrative warning first).

Who must comply

Section 19K (19יא) of the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law says "a public service shall be accessible to persons with disabilities", and whoever is responsible for such a service must make accessibility adjustments. "Public service" is defined in section 19J (19י): a service given to the public, or to an indefinite part of it, by a public body or in a public place, or a service listed in the Law's Second Schedule.

The details are in the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities (Service Accessibility Adjustments) Regulations, 2013. The part on websites and apps is called "internet services" (regulations 35 to 35F), and most of it was added by an amendment in the Hebrew year 5778 (2017–18).

Under regulation 35, an "internet service" is content in any form, such as text, documents, images or video, shown on a website, in a document or in an app, "intended to provide the user with a public service or information about such a service". So a site that only presents information about a business's service can be covered too.

According to Kol Zchut, the duty falls on whoever actually operates the site, not on whoever built it.

What you need to do

  • Website: adjustments per IS 5568 at level AA (regulation 35A(a)). Part 1 of the standard is identical to WCAG 2.0, with national changes. For example, section headings (2.4.10) are required at level AA, and the "language of parts" criterion (3.1.2) doesn't apply.
  • Documents: a document, such as a PDF, prepared from 26 October 2017 onwards and uploaded to the site must be accessible (regulation 35A(c)). IS 5568 part 2 covers documents.
  • App: per an Israeli standard for apps if one exists. If not, per the web standard's criteria as far as possible, on at least two common operating systems (regulation 35C). An app is exempt if the same service exists on a mobile-friendly site that meets the requirements, and the app links to it.
  • Video: the duty to caption pre-recorded videos applies to public authorities and to those with average turnover above ₪5 million (regulation 35D).
  • Other people's content: you aren't responsible for content uploaded by users or third parties that you don't edit, but you must provide accessible infrastructure, such as a field for image descriptions (regulation 35B).
  • Accessibility statement: in a prominent place on the site (regulation 35E). See how to write an accessibility statement.
  • Accessibility coordinator: whoever is responsible for a public service and employs at least 25 employees must appoint one (section 19MB (19מב) of the Law).

The exemptions

The exemptions for websites and apps are in regulation 35F (35ו). Some depend on turnover, so check yours with your accountant.

  1. VAT-exempt dealer, or average turnover up to ₪100,000 (regulation 35F(g)): exempt from the adjustments in the internet services part. "Average turnover" is the average over the last three tax years in which turnover wasn't zero (regulation 35).
  2. Average turnover up to ₪1,000,000 and an older site (regulation 35F(i)): exempt for a site or app you started operating before the regulations took effect, provided the ways to contact you for the service are published accessibly on it. The exemption is valid for three years from the end of the last tax year checked, and can be renewed every three years. According to Kol Zchut, this means a site that operated before 26 October 2017. A new site isn't covered.
  3. Technological exemption (regulation 35F(a) to (c)): a licensed service-accessibility expert may exempt a specific adjustment, based on a professional opinion, if the platform can't support it for technological reasons. It lasts up to three years and can be renewed for up to three more; beyond that only the Commissioner or someone on their behalf, for special reasons. Whoever gets it must provide alternative access and publish the exemption in the accessibility statement.
  4. Social networks (regulation 35F(d)): exempt from a specific adjustment in content published on a social network, if the network doesn't allow it.
  5. Closed services (regulation 35F(e)): if access is limited to pre-registered users and at most 500 participants are registered at the same time, the content needn't be made accessible unless a person with a disability asks.
  6. Advertisements (regulation 35F(j)): the rules don't apply to ads, as long as there is an accessible alternative to the service information in them.

What about turnover of ₪100,000 to ₪300,000? Some information sites describe a renewable exemption for that range. In the text of the regulations I checked (regulation 35F(h)), the extension up to ₪300,000 applied only until 26 October 2020. If that's your situation, check with a lawyer what applies to you today.

There are also separate regulations on exemption for an "undue burden": the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities (Exemption for Undue Burden from Adjustments in an Existing Building and in Public Service) Regulations, 2019.

What happens if the site isn't accessible

Notice, and 60 days to fix

Under regulation 35A(d)(1), a deviation from the internet services rules isn't treated as a breach unless you were sent a notice requiring a fix and didn't fix it within a reasonable time, and no later than 60 days from receiving the notice. Meanwhile you must provide an alternative as soon as possible (regulation 35A(d)(2)), such as the same service by phone. How courts read this provision is a question for a lawyer.

Civil lawsuits

Under section 19NA (19נא) of the Law, breaching the accessibility provisions is a civil tort. A court may award up to ₪50,000 without proof of damage. That amount is updated each year by the consumer price index, against the February 2005 index, so the actual amount today is higher. The Law also lists cases where a court won't award compensation without proof of damage, such as when the defendant is complying with an accessibility order, or when a lawsuit on the same matter was already filed against them, under the conditions in the Law.

Administrative fines from the Commission

Under chapter 9 of the Law, added by a 2022 amendment, the officer at the Commission for Equal Rights of Persons with Disabilities may impose an administrative fine for breaches listed in the Law's Eighth Schedule (section 26G). Two of them concern websites:

A warning first, for smaller businesses: under section 26R(a)(3) (26יח(א)(3)) of the Law, until 10 August 2027 (a four-year temporary provision from the start of chapter 9), when a business with annual turnover up to ₪20 million that isn't a public authority breaches a specific accessibility duty for the first time, the officer issues an administrative warning instead of a fine. A fine can follow if the breach continues or repeats (section 26T (26כ)). Regulations under section 26L (26יב) also allow reduced amounts in some cases.

For a continuing breach, one fiftieth of the amount is added for each month, and a repeat breach within two years doubles it (section 26K). Before a fine, the officer sends a notice of intent, and you have 30 days to submit arguments (sections 26H and 26I).

What to do now

  1. Check your average turnover with your accountant, to see whether a turnover exemption applies.
  2. Run a free automated accessibility check on your main pages, to see the basic problems.
  3. Fix what it finds, and add a short manual check.
  4. Publish an accessibility statement with a contact method someone actually answers.
  5. If you received a notice or letter about an accessibility problem, talk to a lawyer. Under regulation 35A(d)(1), the time to fix runs from the day you received the notice.

Automated checks find only part of accessibility issues. This is not a compliance certificate and not legal advice.

Common questions

Does a small business need an accessible website?

It depends on turnover. A VAT-exempt dealer, or anyone with average annual turnover up to ₪100,000, is exempt (regulation 35F(g)). With average turnover up to ₪1 million and a site that operated before the regulations took effect, a renewable temporary exemption may apply (regulation 35F(i)). Above that the duty applies, unless another exemption does.

Who is responsible: the site owner or the developer?

According to Kol Zchut, whoever actually operates the site. Your contract with the developer is a separate matter between you.

How much is the fine for an inaccessible website?

There is no automatic fine. The Commission may impose an administrative fine: for 2026, ₪3,940 to ₪41,030 for an inaccessible site or app depending on turnover, and ₪1,310 to ₪13,680 for a missing accessibility statement. Until 10 August 2027, a business with turnover up to ₪20 million gets an administrative warning instead of a fine for a first breach (section 26R(a)(3)). Separately, a court may award up to ₪50,000 without proof of damage in a lawsuit, an amount updated by the price index.

Does an accessibility widget exempt me?

I found no such exemption in the Law or the regulations. The regulations require the content itself to meet the standard. I explain this in my guide on accessibility widgets.

Does the duty apply to a site that doesn't sell anything?

The definition in regulation 35 covers content meant to provide a public service "or information about such a service". So a site that presents a service to the public can be covered even without online sales.
